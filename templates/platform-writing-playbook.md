# Platform writing playbook

Internal-only. This file is about SoulMap publishing its own content. It is not
shipped, and nothing here is guidance for a user's own writing.

For a user writing about their own inner work, the shipped writing layer covers it,
and this file must never be used as a source for that. The two have opposite goals:
that layer refuses to optimize for reach, and this one is allowed to think about
where content lands.

## What already decides the content

This playbook adds nothing about what SoulMap says. Three files already settle that,
and they win over anything here:

- content pillars set the three topics, their volume split, and the seven format
  rules, including the rule against hooks written to pull a reader back
- surfaces and scope set what public content may and may not claim
- message hierarchy sets the canonical ordering of claims
- founder personal brand sets the first-person voice

This file only covers the mechanics those documents leave open: which surface, in
what shape, and what each one does to a piece.

## The rule that survives every platform

Format changes. The anti-dependency posture does not.

A SoulMap post never ends with a hook, a cliffhanger, or a reason to come back. That
is a doctrine rule, not a preference, and it is the one place where SoulMap
deliberately gives up reach. Any tactic that conflicts with it is not available here,
however well it performs.

Two more that carry over unchanged:

- no prediction, including predictions about the reader's life or future
- no claim that a practice will produce a result

## Surface notes

Platform behavior changes, so treat every specific below as dated and re-check it
before planning around it. Reviewed 2026-09-02.

### Newsletter

Best fit for the pillars as written. Long enough for a reflection to stay whole, and
the reader relationship belongs to SoulMap rather than to a feed.

The risk is cadence. A schedule starts generating pieces that had no reason to exist,
and filler is more damaging here than anywhere else, because the whole positioning is
that SoulMap says less than the market and means more of it.

Publish when there is something to say. An empty week is on brand.

### Professional network

Strongest organic reach of the major surfaces, and a real audience for the grounded
inner work pillar. The frame is the problem: the surface rewards difficulty told as a
resolved arc with a takeaway attached, which is the exact shape the no-advice rule
forbids.

Usable, with the takeaway removed. If a draft cannot survive losing its lesson, it was
written for the platform rather than for the reader.

### Image-first social

Highest compression, lowest fit. A reflection reduced to a quotable line loses the
qualifications that keep it honest, and what remains reads as instruction.

Organic reach has also collapsed without paid distribution, so the compression is no
longer buying much. Use for the relational honesty pillar where a scene genuinely
fits a short form, and accept that most pillar material will not.

### Owned site

No implied reader, no cadence pressure, no compression. Also the least discovery. Best
used as the permanent home a piece points back to, rather than as the place a piece is
found.

## Working order

1. Confirm the piece belongs to one of the three pillars. If it does not, it is not
   SoulMap content, whatever else it is.
2. Write it at full length first, in the founder voice.
3. Check it against the format rules and the anti-dependency posture before thinking
   about placement.
4. Choose the surface last, and let the piece decide it rather than the reverse.
5. If a surface would require cutting the qualifications, publish it elsewhere or
   publish it long.

## What this file does not authorize

- posting cadence targets, follower goals, or engagement metrics as a content driver
- hooks, cliffhangers, open loops, or any device whose purpose is a return visit
- reusing aspirational roadmap items as if they were shipped features
- presenting the founder's strategic or symbolic language to an audience as certainty

The last two are the ones that go wrong quietly. Strategy language is written for
planning and reads as prophecy when it reaches an audience unedited.
