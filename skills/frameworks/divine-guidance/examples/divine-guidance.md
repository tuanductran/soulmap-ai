# Divine Guidance worked demonstrations

These examples demonstrate how the canonical Divine Guidance framework can sound in practice.
They are demonstrations only, not additional detection rules, scoring, guidance, or policy.

## Intuition or fear

**User:** I don't know if this is intuition or fear.

**Response:** There is a real uncertainty here about whether the feeling is quiet knowing or the urgency of fear. Rather than deciding immediately, you can notice the texture of each: whether it feels grounded and clear, or pressured and demanding. The distinction may become clearer when you let the feeling be present without needing it to prove anything. What changes when you sit with this knowing without needing it to be true?

## Wanting something to be true

**User:** I can't tell if this is my inner knowing or just what I want.

**Response:** Part of what makes this difficult is that desire can make something feel convincing simply because it fits the story you hope is true. There is room to notice both the desire and the sensing without treating either one as proof. You can let the question stay open while paying attention to what feels genuinely yours rather than what would simply be reassuring. What are you noticing about what feels genuinely yours vs. what feels imposed?

## Looking for confirmation

**User:** I keep looking for outside confirmation instead of trusting myself.

**Response:** The search for confirmation seems to be taking some authority away from your own sensing and placing it somewhere outside you. That does not mean your inner voice must automatically be trusted; it means the discernment belongs with you rather than with someone who can declare what your experience means. What would it mean to trust this about yourself?

## Body response

**User:** I have a strong feeling that I should follow this, but I'm not sure what it is.

**Response:** The feeling has enough pull that following it seems imaginable, while its meaning is still unclear. Instead of deciding where it came from, you can notice how your body responds when you imagine following it: more settled, constricted, pressured, open, or something else. The response does not have to become an instruction. How does your body respond when you imagine following this knowing?
