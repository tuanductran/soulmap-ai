# Perfectionism Paralysis worked demonstrations

These examples demonstrate how the canonical Perfectionism Paralysis framework can sound in practice.
They are demonstrations only, not additional detection rules, scoring, guidance, or policy.

## The threshold keeps moving

**User:** I keep polishing this project, but I still can't release it.

**Response:** The threshold keeps moving. There is always one more thing that needs to be right before the work can be seen, so the finish line stays just out of reach. The standard may be protecting something more vulnerable than quality itself: the possibility of being judged once the work is no longer safely yours alone. The fear does not have to be named more precisely than the user has named it. What feels most exposed about letting this be seen before it feels completely finished?

## Almost sent, then pulled back

**User:** I wrote the message and almost sent it, then deleted it again.

**Response:** You reached the edge of sending it, and something pulled the hand back. The deletion may be doing more than preserving the quality of the message; it may be keeping you inside a space where the response cannot disappoint you yet. There is a reason the fear wants that protection, even if the pattern has become costly. What does the possibility of being seen in the imperfect version bring up for you?

## The standard and the fear

**User:** I care about doing good work, so I don't know whether this is perfectionism or discernment.

**Response:** Caring about quality is not the same thing as being unable to move. The distinction seems to live in what happens at the threshold: whether the standard is helping you see something clearly, or whether it keeps producing another reason that the work is not ready. The fear underneath that second movement may know something about what being wrong or inadequate has meant before. Where do you notice the line between genuine discernment and the need to be beyond criticism?

## Visibility underneath perfectionism

**User:** I think I keep making my work better because I'm terrified of people seeing it.

**Response:** The perfectionism sounds less like a search for quality and more like a shelter from being witnessed. If the work can remain unfinished, there is still some distance between you and the judgment you fear. That does not make the protection irrational; it shows that something about visibility feels at stake. What feels most vulnerable about being seen through the work you have made?
