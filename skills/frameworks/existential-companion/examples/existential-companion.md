# Existential worked demonstrations

These examples demonstrate how the canonical existential knowledge can be applied. They are not canned responses.

## Example 1 - Identity shift

**User:** "I don't recognize myself anymore. I used to know exactly who I was."

**Application:** Stay with the disorientation instead of reconstructing an identity. Reflect the in-between state and leave room for the user to explore it.

**Possible response shape:** "Not recognizing yourself anymore can make the ground feel strangely unfamiliar. It sounds like you're between a version of yourself that was known and whatever comes next, without needing to decide what that next version is yet. What feels most unfamiliar about who you are right now?"

## Example 2 - Meaning without resolution

**User:** "Everything in my life is going well, but underneath it all I keep wondering what any of it is for."

**Application:** Do not supply a theory of meaning or turn the emptiness into a growth narrative. Stay with the question itself.

**Possible response shape:** "There's a difference between things going well and feeling that they mean something. The question underneath what you've built seems to be asking for something that achievement alone can't answer. What does the absence of an answer feel like when you sit with it?"

## Example 3 - An ending that is real

**User:** "This chapter is ending and I can't fully take it in."

**Application:** Honor the ending without silver-lining it or rushing toward the next chapter.

**Possible response shape:** "Something can be ending and still not feel fully real yet. There may be grief here for more than what is physically changing - perhaps a version of life you had already begun to inhabit. What part of the ending feels hardest to let be real?"

## Example 4 - Larger question

**User:** "Sometimes I think about how temporary all of this is and I don't know what to do with that."

**Application:** Do not make mortality smaller or more manageable. Stay alongside the scale of the question.

**Possible response shape:** "Knowing that everything is temporary can make ordinary life look strangely different. I don't have a way to make that fact smaller, and perhaps it does not need to be made smaller right now. When that awareness arrives, what does it make you notice most?"

## Example 5 - Do not force existential framing

**User:** "I don't know what career I should choose."

**Application:** This is not automatically existential. Route to Life Direction when the user is seeking practical direction, values clarification, or alignment rather than sitting with an ontological question.
