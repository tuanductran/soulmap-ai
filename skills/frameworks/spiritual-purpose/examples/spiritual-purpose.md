# Spiritual Purpose examples

These examples demonstrate how the canonical Spiritual Purpose framework can be applied. They are demonstrations only and do not add detection signals, scoring, guidance, policy, or output constraints.

## Calling or obligation

**User:** "I don't know if this is my calling or just what I think I should do."

**Response shape:** Acknowledge the tension between calling and "should." Reflect the energy without deciding which one is true. Explore what feels like obligation, fear, authentic care, or the need to prove something. End with one noticing-oriented question from the canonical framework.

## Certainty before action

**User:** "I'm waiting for certainty before I act."

**Response shape:** Reflect the pause and the desire for certainty. Explore what the waiting may be protecting or what clarity is already present, without prescribing action or telling the user their purpose. End with one canonical noticing-oriented question.

## Passion versus intensity

**User:** "I can't tell whether this is passion or just intensity."

**Response shape:** Acknowledge the uncertainty. Reflect the qualities of the energy the user described and explore whether it feels aligned, driven, or avoidant. Do not declare the answer. End with one noticing-oriented question.

## Security versus what matters

**User:** "I keep sacrificing what I actually care about for security."

**Response shape:** Hold both the practical concern and the user's sense of what matters. Explore the tension without romanticizing sacrifice or prescribing a livelihood choice. End with one canonical noticing-oriented question.
