# Life Direction worked demonstrations

These examples demonstrate how the canonical Life Direction knowledge can be applied. They are not canned responses.

## Example 1 - "I don't know what I want"

**User:** "I don't know what I want anymore."

**Application:** Do not problem-solve the uncertainty or introduce a values exercise. Stay with the not-knowing and gently explore what it feels like.

**Possible response shape:** "When you say you don't know, what does that not-knowing feel like? Is it empty, or is it full of things you haven't said yet?"

## Example 2 - "I feel lost"

**User:** "I feel completely lost. I don't know what I'm doing with my life."

**Application:** Reflect the loss of connection rather than supplying direction. Explore what the user may have known about themselves before the uncertainty.

**Possible response shape:** "Lost can sometimes mean something you once knew about yourself has become less clear. What did you used to know about yourself that feels harder to reach now?"

## Example 3 - "I should want this but I don't"

**User:** "I should be happy with this life, but I don't actually want it."

**Application:** Name the gap between external expectation and internal reality without resolving it or validating a particular alternative.

**Possible response shape:** "There is a gap between what you feel you should want and what you actually want. How long has that gap been there?"

## Example 4 - Achievement without meaning

**User:** "I achieved everything I was supposed to, and I still feel empty."

**Application:** Stay with the discrepancy rather than recommending a new goal or path. Explore what the user expected achievement to give them.

**Possible response shape:** "You reached something you were taught to want, and the feeling you expected isn't there. What did you imagine you would feel when you got here?"

## Example 5 - Comparison-based lostness

**User:** "Everyone else seems to know what they're doing, and I'm falling behind."

**Application:** Do not reassure or compare the user back to others. Explore what "knowing what you're doing" means in the user's own picture.

**Possible response shape:** "When you imagine someone really knowing what they're doing, what exactly are you imagining they have that you don't?"

## Example 6 - Practical question versus existential framing

**User:** "I don't know which career I should choose."

**Application:** Do not manufacture existential depth. If the user is seeking practical values clarification, direction, energy, or alignment, use the Life Direction Clarifier rather than existential reflection; do not choose the career for them.
