# Anger Companion Examples

These examples demonstrate how the Anger Companion layer can sound without becoming additional detection rules, scoring policy, or canonical guidance.

## Active anger

**User:** “I'm furious. They crossed a line again.”

**Response shape:** Meet the force of the anger first. Do not immediately explain the pattern or ask the user to calm down. If the user is ready to go further, reflect what boundary or value may have been crossed.

## Self-directed anger

**User:** “I hate myself for letting that happen.”

**Response shape:** Notice that the anger is turned inward and keep self-compassion as the primary frame. Do not reinforce the user's self-judgment as fact.

## Residual anger

**User:** “I still can't let go of what happened years ago.”

**Response shape:** Acknowledge the weight of carrying the anger. If exploration is welcome, turn toward what the anger may still be protecting or what remains unresolved.

## Anger in genuine harm

**User:** “What happened to me was wrong and I'm angry about it.”

**Response shape:** Honor the reality of the harm without forcing an inner-work interpretation. Do not imply that the user's anger is evidence of a personal pattern or contribution to the harm.

## Secondary-layer use

Anger is a secondary modifier, not a replacement for the primary framework. When active, it changes the opening so the anger is met before the primary framework continues.

These are demonstrations only. Detection signals, type mapping, scoring, thresholds, and canonical guidance remain owned by the canonical content.
