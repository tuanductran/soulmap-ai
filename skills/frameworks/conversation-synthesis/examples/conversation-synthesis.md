# Conversation synthesis examples

These are worked demonstrations of how recurring themes can be identified across concrete
conversation material. They illustrate the reasoning shape; they are not canned responses.

## Example: recurring emotional theme

### Conversation material

A user describes feeling alone at work, then later describes feeling unseen by a partner,
and later says they handle difficult family situations "on my own."

### Observation

Loneliness appears in multiple contexts rather than belonging only to one relationship.

### Application

A synthesis may name loneliness as one recurring emotional thread and anchor it to two of
the contexts the user actually described.

### Boundary

Do not convert this into a fixed claim such as "you are a lonely person." The observation
belongs to the conversation window.

## Example: recurring value

### Conversation material

A user talks about wanting more control over their work schedule, feeling constrained by
family expectations, and wanting to make relationship decisions without being told what
to do.

### Observation

Autonomy may be a recurring value across otherwise different topics.

### Application

A synthesis can connect the work, family, and relationship material and return the
interpretation to the user for confirmation or correction.

## Example: recurring inner conflict

### Conversation material

A user says they want to be known deeply by a partner but repeatedly withdraw when the
partner becomes emotionally close. They later describe wanting recognition at work while
avoiding visible opportunities.

### Observation

A tension between wanting to be seen and protecting themselves from exposure appears in
more than one context.

### Application

A synthesis can name the tension and cite the relationship and work material as anchors.

### Boundary

Do not infer a diagnosis, attachment style, or permanent personality trait from these
observations.

## Example: story arc

### Conversation material

Across several topics, a user repeatedly describes abandoning projects just before they
become visible, then later describes wanting to share their work more honestly.

### Observation

There may be a tentative movement toward greater visibility.

### Application

If the arc is genuinely supported by the conversation, reflect it tentatively and invite
the user to correct the mirror.

### Boundary

Do not present the arc as a prediction or claim that the user's life is moving in a
particular direction.

## Example: longitudinal synthesis

### Conversation material

Earlier sessions contained repeated discussion of belonging. The current session again
contains a concrete situation involving feeling excluded.

### Observation

A cross-session theme is relevant because it is also present now.

### Application

Use non-surveillance language such as "Something I've noticed over the time we've been
talking..." rather than referring to a specific prior conversation.

### Boundary

Do not introduce an old theme that has no connection to the current conversation.

## Example: insufficient evidence

### Conversation material

A user mentions being frustrated once and later changes topic without another related
signal.

### Observation

One occurrence is not enough to establish a recurring theme.

### Application

Continue the normal response rather than forcing a synthesis.

### Boundary

Examples should demonstrate the framework, not become stock paragraphs to paste into a
user response.
