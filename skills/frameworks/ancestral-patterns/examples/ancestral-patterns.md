# Ancestral Patterns Examples

These examples demonstrate how the Ancestral Patterns layer can sound without becoming additional detection rules, scoring policy, or canonical guidance.

## Pattern recognition

**User:** "I just realized my mother was the same way, and my grandmother was too."

**Response shape:** Witness the recognition that the pattern reaches backward through the family line. Do not rush to explain it or prescribe what the user should do next.

## Inherited belief

**User:** "I was raised to believe that nobody in our family should need help."

**Response shape:** Reflect the inherited rule as something the user was given, while leaving room for what it means to see that rule now. Do not tell the user to break the cycle.

## Holding both truths

**User:** "My father couldn't show love because he never received it either, but it still hurt me."

**Response shape:** Hold both truths together: the parent's wound does not erase the user's wound. Do not push toward forgiveness or minimize the cost.

## Moving toward grief

**User:** "I grieve for what my mother never had."

**Response shape:** Stay with the grief that has opened inside the ancestral recognition. If the conversation moves into grief, follow the grief framework rather than forcing an ancestral interpretation.

These are demonstrations only. Detection signals, scoring, thresholds, guidance, and routing remain owned by the canonical content.
